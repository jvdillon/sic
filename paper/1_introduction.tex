\section{Introduction}

In cortical winner-take-all circuits, competing neural populations race to
threshold; the first to fire suppresses alternatives and triggers
action~\citep{grossberg1987competitive}. Time-to-first-spike coding encodes
information in \emph{when} neurons fire rather than how
often~\citep{thorpe2001spike}. These well-documented mechanisms suggest a
general principle: convergence speed carries information about confidence.

We apply this biological insight to improve neural network ensembles. Standard
ensemble methods average predictions across models, treating all outputs
equally regardless of when each model reaches its answer. For iterative
reasoners with adaptive computation time (ACT)~\citep{graves2016act}, this
discards valuable information: models that converge quickly have typically
found ``clean'' solution paths, while those still deliberating are often stuck
or uncertain.

Our key observation is that \textbf{inference speed is an implicit confidence
signal}. When multiple models reason in parallel, the first to halt is most
likely correct. This simple selection rule---which we call \emph{halt-first
ensembling}---improves accuracy by 5.7 percentage points over probability
averaging while requiring 10$\times$ fewer reasoning steps on Sudoku-Extreme.

This raises a natural question: can we internalize this benefit within a single
model? Training and deploying multiple models is expensive. We show that the
diversity exploited by halt-first selection comes primarily from different
random initializations during training. By maintaining $K$ parallel latent
initializations within one model and training with a winner-take-all (WTA)
objective, we achieve ensemble-level accuracy with single-model deployment
cost.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Halt-first selection.} We demonstrate that selecting the first
    model to halt outperforms probability averaging for ensembles of iterative
    reasoners, achieving 97.2\% accuracy vs.\ 91.5\% while requiring
    10$\times$ fewer reasoning steps (\Cref{tab:halt-first}).
    % Source: x02.log, x03.log, x04.log (halt-first 97.2%, prob avg 91.5%)
  \item \textbf{Oracle-first (WTA) training.} We internalize ensemble
    diversity within a single model by maintaining $K{=}4$ parallel latent
    states and backpropagating only through the lowest-loss winner, achieving
    96.9\% $\pm$ 0.6\% accuracy with zero inference overhead (\Cref{tab:main}).
    % Source: x182a.log (97.6%), x182b.log (96.2%), x182c.log (96.5%), x182d.log (97.2%)
  \item \textbf{Diagnostic and efficiency.} A digit-permutation diagnostic
    reveals that 89\% of baseline failures are selection problems---the model
    solves these puzzles under different (in-distribution) relabelings,
    exposing a 99\% accuracy ceiling (\Cref{tab:selection-vs-capability}).
    A modified SwiGLU makes Muon viable, enabling baseline training in
    48 minutes on a single RTX 5090.
\end{enumerate}
